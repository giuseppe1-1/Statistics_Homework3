1Model and parameters

There are n systems and m attackers. Attacker j probes the systems one after the other, and Xj,i is its penetration score on system i. The scores are i.i.d. across systems and independent across attackers. Two models are compared:

  • Bernoulli: X ∈ {0, 1} with P(X = 1) = p. The attacker either penetrates the system (1) or not (0), so p is the success probability and the drift is positive.
  • Rademacher: X ∈ {−1, +1} with probability 1/2 each. Progress (+1) and setback (−1) are equally likely: a symmetric, zero-mean contest between attack and defence.

For one attacker the three quantities studied are the total score Sn = X1 + … + Xn, the average score Sn/n (success rate per system) and the normalized total score Sn/√n. The m attackers give m independent copies of each, from which the distributions are estimated. All values of n are read off the same trajectories (the walk is simulated up to the largest n, and S is recorded at every n in the list), so the n-dependence is that of a real stochastic process.

Observe how the same n gives three very different pictures. Sn is wide and keeps widening, Sn/n is narrow and concentrates, Sn/√n has a fixed width. In the Bernoulli model the distribution of Sn/√n is a bell of unchanged width whose centre p√n runs away, while Zn stays fixed at N(0,1).

4Law of Large Numbers: convergence in probability of Sn/n

Convergence in probability means that, for every ε > 0, P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) → 0, where μ = p (Bernoulli) or 0 (Rademacher). The tables compare the frequency observed among the m attackers with the exact binomial value and with three theoretical estimates: the Chebyshev bound σ²/(nε²), the Hoeffding bound 2e−2nε² (Bernoulli) or 2e−nε²/2 (Rademacher), and the normal approximation from the CLT.

An observed frequency of 0 only means that no attacker out of m was outside the tube, so the true probability is below about 1/m; on the charts it is drawn on the bottom edge. The Chebyshev bound is valid but very loose (it only decays like 1/n), Hoeffding is exponentially small, and the exact value is matched very closely by the simulation and by the normal approximation once n is moderately large. The convergence is also slower when σ is larger: compare the two models at the same n.

5Variance under the different normalizations

Dividing by 1, by n or by √n has a precise effect on the spread: Var[Sn] = nσ² grows, Var[Sn/n] = σ²/n vanishes, and Var[Sn/√n] = σ² is constant. Only the last normalization gives a non-degenerate limit.

The sample variance is computed over the m attackers with the unbiased estimator; its relative error is about √(2/m), so ratios close to 1 within a few percent are what is expected. On the log-log chart the three theoretical lines have slopes +1, −1 and 0.

6Central Limit Theorem: convergence in distribution to N(0,1)

The histograms show the standardized score Zn for every n in the list, with the standard normal density. For the Rademacher model this is exactly Sn/√n. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance Dn = supx |Fn(x) − Φ(x)| quantifies the gap between the empirical distribution and N(0,1).

Rademacher model, Sn/√n

Two effects limit what can be seen. For small n the distribution of Sn lives on a lattice, so its distance from a continuous law is at least half the largest jump, of order 1/√n: this is the same order as the Berry–Esseen bound Cρ/(σ³√n) with C = 0.4748, and the measured distance decreases at the same 1/√n rate until it reaches the noise floor. For large n the true distance falls below the sampling noise 1.36/√m of an empirical distribution built from m attackers: at that point Dn can no longer be distinguished from 0 and stops decreasing, which says something about m rather than about n. Skewness and excess kurtosis (columns on the right) approach 0 at rates 1/√n and 1/n for the Bernoulli model; in the Rademacher model the skewness is 0 by symmetry and only the kurtosis, −2/n, is non-trivial.

7Interpretation: system size and number of attackers

The role of n, the number of systems

  • Absolute score. An attacker facing n systems collects a total Sn with mean nμ and standard deviation σ√n. In the Bernoulli model the score is dominated by the drift: for example, with p = 0.3 and n = 10,000 about 3,000 systems fall and the uncertainty (about 46 systems) is small in comparison. In the Rademacher model there is no drift: the attacker is as likely to be ahead as behind, and the typical distance from 0 grows like √n.
  • Relative score. The fraction of compromised systems Sn/n concentrates around p (LLN), with fluctuations of order σ/√n. A larger infrastructure therefore makes the outcome more predictable: the defender can plan on the success rate p, and the probability of a result far from it decays exponentially (Hoeffding). In the zero-mean model the net score per system tends to 0, but only at the slow rate 1/√n.
  • Shape of the fluctuations. The CLT says that, on the √n scale, the fluctuations are Gaussian whatever the details of the score. Tail probabilities such as “the attacker gets more than x systems beyond the average” can then be estimated with Φ using only the mean and variance. For small n or p close to 0 or 1 the approximation is poor (the Bernoulli law is skewed, and with np small it is closer to Poisson), as the skewness column shows.

The role of m, the number of attackers

  • m does not change the law of one attacker. The distribution of Sn for each attacker depends only on n and on the model. What m changes is how well the distribution is estimated: the error of an empirical mean is σ/√m, the distance of an empirical cdf from the true one is about 1/√m, and rare events (probability below 1/m) are simply not observed.
  • Pooling the attackers. If the m attackers act on the same n systems, the pooled score T = ∑j Sn(j) is a sum of mn i.i.d. terms: mean mnμ, standard deviation σ√(mn), so the total grows linearly in m but the relative spread falls like 1/√(mn). More attackers make the aggregate more predictable, in the same way as more systems. This holds as long as the attackers are independent: coordinated attackers, shared vulnerabilities or systems that are not equivalent break the i.i.d. assumption and the √ gain.

Limits of the model

Independent and identically distributed scores are a strong assumption. Real systems differ in strength and share software, so the successes are positively correlated; real attackers learn from earlier attempts, so the probability of success changes along the way. Correlation increases the variance of Sn above nσ² and can destroy the √n scaling and the Gaussian limit, so the results above should be read as the reference behaviour of the independent case.
